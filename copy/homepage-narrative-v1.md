**Concept:** The Signature. The site is a journey in five chapters, from seal to invitation.
**Voice:** Measured, intelligent, direct, unfussy. Composed, not loud. Short sentences. No hype, no exclamation marks.
**Primary action:** Start a project brief.

## Chapter 2 — What the signature means

**Section intro:**
Three disciplines. One standard.

Every project we take on is shaped by the same three forces. They are in our name because they are in our process.

**Creative — Technology that feels alive.**
Design, interaction, and storytelling that make people want to use what we build. Because the best system in the world fails if nobody enjoys touching it.

**Code — Engineering you can build a business on.**
Custom websites, webapps, and apps engineered to be fast, reliable, and maintainable. No shortcuts that become your problem later.

**Logic — Solutions that make sense in the real world.**
Structure, usability, and practical thinking. We build what solves the problem — not what pads the demo.

> Annotation: each pillar is written as a client benefit, not an abstract value. "What does Creative buy me?" is answered in every subhead. Order follows the triquetra's three loops — the visual system and the copy system tell the same story.

## Chapter 3 — Where the signature lives

**Section intro:**
The signature is already out there.

Two live products carry it today. Not mockups. Not concepts. Working systems you can open, click, and judge right now.

**Case block template (×2):**

> ### [Product name]
> **The problem:** [one sentence — what was broken or missing]
> **The thinking:** [two sentences — the key decision that shaped the solution]
> **The craft:** [one or two concrete details — performance, a specific interaction, a technical choice]
>
> [ Live product → ] &nbsp; Built with Creative Code Logic ✦

> Annotation: two real, visitable builds beat ten screenshots of imaginary ones. Keep each case under 60 words — the live link does the convincing. The ✦ seal next to each product repeats the hero device so the visual language stays consistent.

**Optional module — the meta case study:**
This website is project 001.
Everything we ask you to trust — the design, the engineering, the thinking — you're using it right now. [How we built it →]

> Annotation: radical transparency as a credibility play. Nobody expects a new studio to open its own build journal; that's exactly why it works. Can launch later as a journal page and be linked from this module.

## Chapter 4 — How we earn the signature

**Section intro:**
We don't sign everything we build. That's the point.

A project earns the mark when it meets the standard we built the company on:

- **It solves the real problem.** Utility first — not noise, not features for their own sake.
- **Every detail has a reason.** If an element, interaction, or line of code can't justify itself, it goes.
- **It performs.** Speed, reliability, and polish are not finishing touches. They are the product.
- **It adapts.** Built to flex across your brand, your market, and your growth.

**Process strip (4 steps):**
1. **Listen** — we start with your problem, not our portfolio
2. **Think** — structure before pixels; the logic comes first
3. **Build** — designed, engineered, and tested to the standard above
4. **Sign** — shipped only when we're proud to put the name on it

> Annotation: this chapter converts your values page into client-facing standards. Step 4 ("Sign") is the differentiator — it turns a process diagram into a promise, and it only works because the signature was established in Chapter 1.

Tell us what you're trying to make. Three questions, two minutes — and we'll come back with thinking, not a sales call.

**CTA:** Start your brief
**Quiet alternative below:** Or just say hello →

> Annotation: the closer flatters the visitor — their project must be worth your signature. The brief-builder (instead of a contact form) is itself a proof of craft: the first thing they touch is something you built. "Thinking, not a sales call" answers the #1 hesitation of clients approaching a new studio.

**Closer alternatives:**
- A: "Ready to build something worth signing?" — makes the client want to earn the seal
